# German (de) glossary for greycard's window

This is the glossary of a draft translation. A native speaker has
corrected some of its terms; corrections to it and to the catalog are
welcome.

Source of the terms: the German Lightroom Classic (LrC) first, Capture
One (C1) and darktable (dt) where LrC has none or they differ.

## Conventions

- **Address.** Buttons, menu items, and commands: infinitive
  ("Exportieren", "Ordner öffnen..."). A sentence that speaks to the
  user: "Sie". Never "du". Status lines: neutral, nominal or passive
  ("3 Bilder exportiert", "exportiere..." avoided; "wird exportiert...").
- **Capitalization.** German noun capitalization; otherwise sentence
  case as the English has. Section headings in ALL CAPS stay ALL CAPS
  ("WEISSABGLEICH": ß becomes SS in caps, the common convention).
  Status-line fragments that start lowercase in English start lowercase
  unless the first word is a German noun.
- **Product words.** greycard, Lightroom, RawTherapee, darktable,
  lensfun, GitHub, JPEG, TIFF, PNG, DNG, ICC, EXIF, XMP, file names,
  units (MB, px, EV, K), and the search's tokens (`camera:`, `iso>=`)
  stay as they are.
- **Keys.** Key names stay as the keys sheet draws them (Ctrl, Shift,
  Alt, Space, Enter, Esc, Tab, Home, End), as docs/translating.md asks:
  the sheet's chords come from the English keys table, so a German
  "Strg" in a sentence would sit beside "Ctrl+A" in the sheet. The
  pointer's word is translated ("Ctrl+Klick"). A German reviewer may
  prefer Strg/Umschalt/Leertaste/Eingabe throughout, which needs the
  chords themselves translated first.
- **Ellipsis and punctuation.** As the source: "..." stays three dots
  where the source has three dots, "…" where it has "…". A colon gets
  no space before it (German, unlike French). Leading and trailing
  spaces, commas, and semicolons of a tail kept.
- **Numbers and dates** keep the English form (§616).
- **Plurals.** nplurals=2; plural=(n != 1).
- **Frame / picture.** Both "Bild" (LrC says "Foto"; "Bild" is shorter
  and C1 and dt say "Bild").
