# §450. The tone's guide made after the retouch

*2026-10-29.* A fill over a dark radio tower against a plain sky
came out with a bright streak the shape of the tower's stem, 20 px
wide and 9 to 23 percent over the sky at the tower's foot, in the
viewport and the export alike, with the fill itself right: its window
after the fill and the composited region after the grain both sat
within 1 percent of the sky at the stem. With Light's Shadows,
Highlights, Whites, and Blacks at zero the streak was gone to 0.3
percent; sharpen and noise off changed nothing.

The tone equalizer's guide plane (§85, §315), the blurred log
luminance the four adaptive sliders read to decide where to lift and
where to pull, was made from the base after the lens and before the
retouch, on the claim that no patch moves a region's mean luminance
by anything the guide's scale can see. A dark tower removed from a
bright sky does: the guide still held it, and shadows at +1.2 lifted
the filled sky under the old stem as a shadow. §443, §447, and §448
each measured and took out real drifts in the fills, smaller than
this, which is why the streak outlived them.

The guide is now made from the retouched picture: a fresh retouch
rebuilds it from the patched picture it is kept with, a kept retouch
keeps both, and a retouch emptied rebuilds the base's. Every reader,
the viewport, the export, the thumbnails of the open frame, reads the
one guide. The reduced develop that makes thumbnails of frames never
opened had the same order and the same fix; a Fill is never made
there, so its picture and its guide agree either way. A slider drag
pays nothing new; a patch drag pays one guide plane a develop, of
the order of the picture copy the retouch already makes. Opening or
turning a retouched frame makes the guide twice, once for the base
and once after the retouch, a waste of tens of milliseconds left for
now.

Measured on the frame, the stem against the sky beside it: master
+6 to +11 percent in three bands at the foot, after this within 0.5.
